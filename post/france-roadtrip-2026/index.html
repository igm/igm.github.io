Thirteen days is a long time to sit in a car, and still not enough for France. Below, the stops that were worth the detour — and what we’d do the same again.

Detours

Highlights

Étretat was the first “wow” of the trip. We timed the arrival for about 13:00, hiked the cliff path along the top of the white chalk arches and then came down to the beach as the tide dropped, so we saw the formations from both sides within three hours.

The D-Day coast was the day that weighed the most. Pointe du Hoc is still cratered like a moonscape, and the American cemetery above Omaha Beach needs no commentary at all. If you do one stop there, do Colleville-sur-Mer.

Mont-Saint-Michel justifies the 07:30 alarm. Arriving for the 09:00 opening we walked the abbey while it was still quiet; by noon the laneways were a river of people and we were already having crêpes in Dinan.

In Cancale you buy oysters at the market stall right on the quay, straight from the producers, and eat them on the sea wall with a baguette. A dozen for two people costs less than a coffee back home.

The Passage du Gois was the quirkiest drive: a 4 km road that spends most of the day under the Atlantic. We had the tide table printed, waited for the 12:30–14:45 window, and crossed with the sea lapping just below the edge of the pavement.

And Chenonceau closed the trip perfectly: the château that straddles the River Cher, walked in the morning at opening time, then paddled underneath it in kayaks in the afternoon — the best view of the arches is from the water.

Notes for next time

What worked

  • One base per region instead of daily repacking: two nights in Rouen, three in Plouër-sur-Rance, three in Pornic, two in Amboise.
  • Big sights (Mont-Saint-Michel, Chenonceau) at the opening bell — by 11:00 both are a different place.
  • Tide tables printed in advance for Étretat and the Passage du Gois; both stops were planned around them, not the other way round.
  • Transit days kept honest: one proper stop each (Nuremberg, Nancy, Linz) instead of five rushed twenty-minute ones.

À bientôt;, France. 🇫🇷
