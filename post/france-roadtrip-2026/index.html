France Road Trip 2026

August 16, 2026

In late July we pointed the Toyota Verso west and didn’t really stop until the Atlantic. Thirteen days, 3,555 kilometres, four countries: a loop from Bratislava through Normandy and Brittany, down the Atlantic coast to the Loire Valley, and back home via Stuttgart. This post is the trip as we actually drove it — the whole route day by day, plus the highlights worth the detour.

The numbers

  • 13 days on the road (24 July – 5 August 2026)
  • 3,555 km total, more than 40 hours behind the wheel
  • 4 countries: Slovakia, Austria, Germany, France
  • 23 stops on the route, from Verdun to Linz
  • 3 pure transit days, 4 transfer days with a programme, 6 excursion days from a fixed base

The route, day by day

  • Day 1 · Fri 24 Jul — Bratislava → Metz (880 km, 9¼ h). Lunch and a walk through Nuremberg’s old centre; evening along the Moselle in Metz.
  • Day 2 · Sat 25 Jul — Metz → Rouen (~470 km), with stops at the Verdun memorials, Reims cathedral and lunch, and Compiègne, where the 1918 armistice was signed.
  • Day 3 · Sun 26 Jul — Étretat day trip: cliff-top hike in the early afternoon, the arches from the beach at low tide.
  • Day 4 · Mon 27 Jul — Rouen → Plouër-sur-Rance (~300 km) via coffee in Honfleur, then the D-Day coast: Omaha Beach, Pointe du Hoc, the American cemetery at Colleville-sur-Mer and the battery at Longues-sur-Mer. Sunset from Saint-Malo’s ramparts.
  • Day 5 · Tue 28 Jul — Mont-Saint-Michel for the 09:00 opening, medieval Dinan in the afternoon.
  • Day 6 · Wed 29 Jul — the oyster market in Cancale in the morning, Breton beaches in the afternoon.
  • Day 7 · Thu 30 Jul — Plouër-sur-Rance → Pornic (~200 km) via the menhirs of Monteneuf, pretty Rochefort-en-Terre and lunch in walled Vannes.
  • Day 8 · Fri 31 Jul — the WWII submarine base at Saint-Nazaire, then Nantes and Les Machines de l’île, with the giant shopping run at Atlantis to close the day.
  • Day 9 · Sat 1 Aug — Noirmoutier island: beaches, castle and salt marshes, and the tidal causeway Passage du Gois driven in the safe window (12:30–14:45).
  • Day 10 · Sun 2 Aug — Pornic → Amboise (300 km) via Angers; evening stroll under the royal château.
  • Day 11 · Mon 3 Aug — Chenonceau straight at the opening bell, kayaks under the château’s arches in the afternoon, local wine in the evening.
  • Day 12 · Tue 4 Aug — Amboise → Stuttgart (660 km), breaking the drive on Place Stanislas in Nancy; car museum before dinner.
  • Day 13 · Wed 5 Aug — Stuttgart → Bratislava (670 km), lunch and a stretch in Linz, home in the evening.

Highlights

Étretat was the first “wow” of the trip. We timed the arrival for about 13:00, hiked the cliff path along the top of the white chalk arches and then came down to the beach as the tide dropped, so we saw the formations from both sides within three hours.

The D-Day coast was the day that weighed the most. Pointe du Hoc is still cratered like a moonscape, and the American cemetery above Omaha Beach needs no commentary at all. If you do one stop there, do Colleville-sur-Mer.

Mont-Saint-Michel justifies the 07:30 alarm. Arriving for the 09:00 opening we walked the abbey while it was still quiet; by noon the laneways were a river of people and we were already having crêpes in Dinan.

In Cancale you buy oysters at the market stall right on the quay, straight from the producers, and eat them on the sea wall with a baguette. A dozen for two people costs less than a coffee back home.

The Passage du Gois was the quirkiest drive: a 4 km road that spends most of the day under the Atlantic. We had the tide table printed, waited for the 12:30–14:45 window, and crossed with the sea lapping just below the edge of the pavement.

And Chenonceau closed the trip perfectly: the château that straddles the River Cher, walked in the morning at opening time, then paddled underneath it in kayaks in the afternoon — the best view of the arches is from the water.

What worked

  • One base per region instead of daily repacking: two nights in Rouen, three in Plouër-sur-Rance, three in Pornic, two in Amboise.
  • Big sights (Mont-Saint-Michel, Chenonceau) at the opening bell — by 11:00 both are a different place.
  • Tide tables printed in advance for Étretat and the Passage du Gois; both stops were planned around them, not the other way round.
  • Transit days kept honest: one proper stop each (Nuremberg, Nancy, Linz) instead of five rushed twenty-minute ones.

Thirteen days is a long time to sit in a car, and still not long enough for France. À bientôt.
