One Week of Weather

August 29, 2026 · 4 min read

Last week Bratislava ran an experiment on me. I only asked for the weather; I got a textbook lesson in trend lines. Seven days, one heat spike, one thunderstorm and one suspiciously tidy cool-down — plotted, fitted and over-analysed below.

Seven days

The daily highs from last Saturday, as served by the open-meteo API for Bratislava (48.15°N, 17.11°E), rounded to whole degrees:

  • Sat 22. 26°C — overcast, harmless.
  • Sun 23. 25°C — the week’s low key.
  • Mon 24. 26°C — quietly warming.
  • Tue 25. 27°C — the first proper summer day.
  • Wed 26. 28°C — confidence interval: beach.
  • Thu 27. 29°C — the air conditioner earns its keep.
  • Fri 28. 31.5°C — the peak, followed by the storm.

The storm

Friday afternoon ended the streak in style: a band of thunderstorms swept through, dropping about 3.6 mm of rain and cutting the temperature from 31.5°C to 27°C within hours. Classic Central-European August: bake first, rinse later. Saturday then opened at 18°C under partly cloudy skies — nine degrees cooler than the previous afternoon, without a single weather app being believed about it.

Reading the regression

Fit a line through those seven highs and you get roughly +0.9°C per day. The fit is genuinely decent — the week was almost a linear warm-up — which is exactly what makes the ending so instructive. Extrapolation says Sunday should hit 32°C. The forecast says 29.7°C, then 25.7°C with showers on Monday.

Every model is a record of what the atmosphere was doing, not what it owes you. A trend line is a summary of the past wearing the grammar of the future.

python
highs = [26, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 31.5]  # Aug 22–28

slope, intercept = np.polyfit(range(7), highs, 1)
# slope ≈ +0.90 °C/day — and absolutely no
# authority over what happens on day 8.

So what

Two things survive contact with the data. First: never argue with a thunderstorm; it will win the extrapolation argument. Second: the same lesson applies to every chart in every dashboard — a fitted line describes, it does not decree. The atmosphere, like the market, is under no obligation to continue your regression.
